I am a Research Assistant in the [Neurorecovery & Pain Group](https://pain-neurorecovery-group.github.io/) under the supervision of Jan Rosner. I hold an MSc in Psychology: Theory & Research from KU Leuven, where I developed a strong foundation in rigorous scientific methods and research practice.

During my master's internship at the [Embodied Computation Group](https://www.the-ecg.org/), led by Prof. Micah Allen, I developed a strong interest in computational and statistical modeling. Since then, I have applied these methods to study cognition and neurophysiology, with a particular focus on the mechanisms underlying metacognition and the use of computational models to infer motor unit physiology.

My skills
======
During my education, I developed a strong interest in statistics and quantitative methods. I was trained primarily within a frequentist framework, learning how to fit, interpret, and evaluate statistical models alongside the mathematical foundations necessary to understand their assumptions and limitations.

During my Master's internship, I gained experience formulating research questions, translating them into measurable constructs, and selecting appropriate study designs and analytical approaches to answer them. I also expanded into Bayesian modeling, which has since become my primary statistical framework.

I particularly enjoy the technical aspects of model development and inference. My interests lie not only in building models that perform well, but also in ensuring that they accurately reflect the underlying scientific mechanisms and provide meaningful answers to substantive research questions.

Current Projects & Interests
======
I am currently involved in two ongoing research projects.

The first project investigates whether first-order decisions in a two-alternative forced-choice (2AFC) paradigm can serve as an independent source of information during post-decisional evidence accumulation (PDEA) in metacognitive judgments.

The second project focuses on motor unit number estimation. We aim to use CMAP scans in combination with a computational model implemented in Stan to estimate the number of available motor units, balancing clinical practicality with a mechanistic modeling framework.

My primary research interests lie in metacognition, computational modeling, and neurophysiology. Beyond my current research interests, I am also fascinated by pain and learning. I hope to continue exploring these topics throughout my career and expand my research across these domains.
